I have frequently noticed odd differences in how my British partner and I experienced our school years.

The length of the summer break is one example. I grew up in Estonia, where summer holidays meant the entire summer was off: from the 1st of June until the end of August, with school starting back on the 1st of September. My British friends had a completely different experience, with a much shorter break starting later in the summer. You can compare how the rest of Europe handles it on the [summer holidays map](/maps?dataset=summer-holidays).

School uniforms are another big contrast. In Tallinn, my school didn't have a uniform at all, though a select list of Estonian schools required traditional school caps—which I always thought were really cool. The [school uniforms map](/maps?dataset=school-uniforms) shows just how much this varies by country.

School meals are also an interesting point. I didn't have free school lunches growing up in the nineties, but that changed when I was a teenager, and we started getting free hot meals and milk. Today, free school lunches are universal in Estonia. You can see how that compares to other countries [here](/maps?dataset=free-school-meals).

Finally, my partner often mentions having to pray during school assemblies. That was genuinely surprising to me—there was no worship in my school at all. We only touched on religion through history or art classes, with no dedicated RE course. This [map](/maps?dataset=religious-education) explores how religious education is handled across Europe.
